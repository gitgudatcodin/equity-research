## 1. Investment Summary

Celsius Holdings is the clear #3 player in the ~$21–28B U.S. energy drink category with ~20–21% dollar share across a three-brand portfolio (Celsius, Alani Nu, Rockstar), distributed exclusively through PepsiCo. The stock has fallen ~72% from its early-2024 peak near $100 — first on a 2024 PepsiCo inventory destocking episode, then on a Q2 2026 earnings miss driven by a shrinking core Celsius brand and margin compression. The market is pricing the company as if Alani Nu's torrid growth is fleeting and the core brand's decay is permanent.

We disagree. At $28, CELH trades at ~19x consensus FY2026 EPS and ~10x forward EV/EBITDA — a 40–60% discount to Monster (34x forward P/E, ~25–31x EV/EBITDA) — while owning the fastest-growing brand in the category (Alani Nu, +56% tracked-channel retail sales in Q2 2026), a genuine PepsiCo distribution moat, the best caffeine-per-dollar value position among mainstream brands, and an essentially unlevered balance sheet. Our DCF and relative-valuation work points to fair value of ~$36–39; we set a **12-month target of $38** and initiate with a **BUY**.

**What could go wrong:** continued decay of the core Celsius brand (−11.7% revenue in Q2), a promotional price war, PepsiCo concentration risk (~60% of revenue), emerging youth caffeine regulation that hits Alani Nu's demographic hardest, and $2.3B of acquisition goodwill that would impair if influencer-driven brand equity fades.

## 3. What the Company Does

Celsius Holdings is a capital-light "better-for-you" functional beverage platform. It does not manufacture its own drinks at scale; production is outsourced to third-party co-packers (it acquired one contract manufacturer, Big Beverages, for $75M in late 2024 to tighten supply-chain control). Revenue comes from shipments through distributors — overwhelmingly PepsiCo's U.S. direct-store-delivery (DSD) system — plus international license partners and e-commerce (Amazon/DTC).

**History in brief.** The brand began as a 2003 "negative-calorie" thermogenic gimmick, went through a delisting (2010), and was rebuilt under CEO John Fieldly (CFO 2012, CEO 2018) into a fitness-lifestyle identity: zero sugar, fruit-forward, 200mg caffeine, marketed through gym and influencer culture. The 2020–2023 run made it one of the best-performing U.S. stocks. In August 2022 PepsiCo invested $550M in convertible preferred stock (~8.5% stake) and became the primary U.S. distributor.

**2025 was the transformational year.** Celsius closed two acquisitions:

- **Alani Nu — $1.8B (closed April 2025):** $1.275B cash + $500M in shares (~22.5M shares, ~8.7% pro-forma ownership) + $25M earn-out; net $1.65B after ~$150M NPV of tax benefits. Funded with a $900M Term Loan B plus cash on hand (~1.0x pro-forma net leverage). Valuation was <3.0x Alani's $595M 2024 revenue and ~12x fully-synergized EBITDA ($137M). Alani is female/Gen-Z-skewed, influencer-driven, and was the fastest-growing brand in the category while the core Celsius brand cooled.
- **Rockstar Energy — U.S. & Canada (August 2025, from PepsiCo):** rounds out coverage of the conventional, male-skewed, value energy segment. PepsiCo retains Rockstar internationally.

Simultaneously (August 2025), PepsiCo moved Alani Nu onto its DSD system, invested another **$585M in convertible preferred** (stake now ~11%, second board seat), and named Celsius its **"strategic energy lead in the U.S."** — Celsius now manages Celsius, Alani Nu, and Rockstar while PepsiCo distributes the full portfolio.

## 6. Growth and Market Outlook

**Category:** U.S. energy drinks ~$21–28B, growing ~4–8% annually (sources vary by methodology); global ~$92B at ~8% CAGR. Growth is driven by the shift to functional/sugar-free energy, gym-to-street occasions, and Gen-Z digital marketing. It is one of the most recession-resilient CPG categories.

**Share position (company-reported Circana, Q2 2026):** the CELH portfolio holds ~20.1% U.S. RTD energy dollar share — Celsius 9.5%, Alani Nu 8.7% (#4 brand nationally, bigger than Bang, Ghost, or C4 individually), Rockstar 1.9%. Tracked-channel retail sales grew +31% YoY in the latest 13-week period.

**International — the long runway:** only ~3–4% of revenue today ($62.5M in H1 2026, +32%), but growing fast (Q1 +55%). Footprint: Nordics, UK, France, Ireland, Australia/NZ, Benelux; Spain launched March 2026 via Suntory Beverage & Food (exclusive), Portugal next; Dublin global HQ in place; Aston Martin F1 partnership (Jan 2026) as global brand vehicle. Management targets **15%+ of revenue from international by 2030** — if achieved, that alone is a ~$500M+ revenue stream at today's scale.

**2027 catalysts:** new Celsius 16oz line, largest-ever Alani LTO ("Witch's Brew"), deeper international rollout, completed integration → management guides gross margins back toward the low-50s.

## 10. Recommendation

**BUY — 12-month price target $38.00.**

The market has confused integration noise with structural decline. The Q2 miss was driven by deliberate SKU rationalization, promo investment, and club-channel softness in the core brand — while the portfolio still grew tracked-channel retail +31%, held ~20% share, and Alani Nu compounded at +56%. At 19x forward earnings and ~10x forward EBITDA, you are paying a no-growth multiple for a 20%-plus earnings compounder with a clean balance sheet, an activist-free path to deleveraging, and a visible 2027 catalyst calendar (Celsius 16oz line, Witch's Brew LTO, international ramp). The preferred overhang ($1.76B) and PepsiCo concentration are real, but they are already reflected in a price 72% below the 2024 peak.

Position sizing should respect the volatility: this is a high-beta, sentiment-driven name (±15% on earnings is normal). A starter position with room to add on brand-stabilization evidence (Q3/Q4 core-brand retail trends) is the right posture.

## 11. Key Risks

1. **Core brand decay continues** — Celsius −11.7% in Q2; if the 2027 refresh fails, the highest-margin asset keeps shrinking.
2. **Alani Nu durability** — influencer-driven brands can fade fast (see Prime Energy's collapse); $2.3B of goodwill would impair.
3. **Margin pressure** — promo intensity, channel mix, and aluminum/diesel inflation vs. the low-50s gross margin target.
4. **PepsiCo concentration** — ~60% of revenue flows through one partner; the 2024 destocking episode shows the shipment-timing volatility this creates.
5. **Regulation** — England bans >150mg/L sales to under-16s from April 2027; Quebec similar; several U.S. states advancing curbs. Youth-skewed Alani Nu is most exposed.
6. **Overhangs** — a shareholder class action (channel-stuffing allegations), ~8% short interest, and August 2026 activist/leadership noise (COO departure; Rockstar co-founder's 4.7% stake and leadership critique).
