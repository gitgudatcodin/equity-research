02 · Business model

The buy-side control plane for the open internet

Advertisers and agencies use The Trade Desk's demand-side platform to buy and optimize display, video, connected TV, mobile and audio inventory. TTD generally earns a platform fee as a percentage of client spend, plus fees for data and value-added services.

Scale

$13.4B of gross ad spend ran through the platform in 2025, generating $2.896B of revenue. The implied take rate was about 21.6%, though management does not disclose take rate as a KPI and mix or volume discounts can move it.

Positioning

TTD does not own a large consumer-media property. That independence is the core pitch: advertisers can compare inventory across the open internet without buying through a media owner grading its own work.

Concentration

Two agency holding companies represented 30% of 2025 gross platform spend. That scale produces workflow depth, but it also gives a small number of intermediaries meaningful negotiating leverage.

Where growth can come from

Global CTV advertising is projected by Omdia to grow from $44B in 2025 to $81B in 2030. Retail media is projected to exceed $300B by 2030. TTD's international mix remains only 18% as of Q1 2026, while EMEA and APAC each grew about 30% year to date in Q2.

Where economics can weaken

A shift toward programmatic-guaranteed and fixed-price deals reduces the value of real-time decisioning. Amazon can combine commerce data, owned inventory and a DSP, while offering discounts. Those pressures can compress both spend share and the 20–21% historical take-rate zone.

03 · Financial condition

A strong income statement meets a sharp growth reset

From 2020 through 2025, revenue compounded at roughly 28% annually. The 2026 cadence changed the debate: Q1 grew 11.8%, Q2 just 3%, and Q3 guidance points to contraction.

04 · Product evaluation

A strong buying stack — with proof points still uneven

Kokai + Zuma

Core franchise

Kokai applies AI to planning, decisioning and measurement. All clients were using it by August 2026, though that does not prove full default migration. Reported outcomes include a 26% CPA improvement and 94% higher CTR; Zuma's initial claim is 32% average CPA improvement. These are company claims, not independently audited benchmarks. A buyer told Digiday the platform may still be catching up on AI features.

Best asset
Must prove monetization and speed

OpenPath

Strategic, contested

A direct buyer-to-publisher route intended to improve supply transparency. Xumo and Massarius expanded access in 2026, but WPP and Dentsu reportedly exited amid fee and transparency concerns. It improves control of the supply path while risking channel conflict.

Mixed
Useful route, agency friction

UID2 / EUID

Broad adoption

An open-source identity framework based on hashed and salted email. Major participants include NBCUniversal, Paramount, Disney/Hulu, Roku and Spotify. But TTD is the sole protocol administrator, and a broken-token incident at a major CTV publisher went undetected for three months with no measurable revenue effect.

Promising
Governance and efficacy questions

Audience Unlimited

Near-term lever

The data-marketplace redesign gives advertisers more control over audiences and fees. Reported Control Mode fees are 3.3% or 4.4% of impression cost, while Performance Mode is free. It can make data economics clearer, but the value still depends on campaign outcomes.

Constructive
Cleaner data economics

Ventura OS

Option value only

The smart-TV operating-system initiative aims to improve CTV monetization and transparency. DIRECTV announced a custom version, and the Ventura Ecosystem added initial collaborators. There is no verified evidence in the research of 2026 TV shipments or revenue contribution.

Unproven
Do not value as a current driver
05 · Competition & moat

Independence is differentiation, not immunity

The core strategic advantage is neutrality across publishers and channels. The core strategic weakness is that the largest rivals can pair proprietary consumer data with owned inventory and cheaper automation.

Durable advantages

  • Buyer-only positioning and objective measurement
  • Open-internet and CTV breadth
  • Cross-channel decisioning and data interoperability
  • Deep agency workflow integration
  • >95% annual customer retention
versus

Eroding defenses

  • Amazon's commerce signals and owned supply
  • Google/Meta automation and proprietary identity
  • Agency multi-homing and fee leverage
  • Take-rate pressure from lower-cost alternatives
  • Morningstar's moat rating cut to “none”

Earnings lens

At $13.92, the shares trade at roughly 11.4× FY2026 adjusted EPS consensus. That looks low, but FY2026 and FY2027 estimates were sharply cut and the latter is slightly lower at $1.20.

Enterprise-value lens

Estimated EV/revenue of 1.75× and EV/adjusted EBITDA of 4.5× reflect a major de-rating. These figures are derived from the market snapshot and balance-sheet liquidity, not company-defined valuation KPIs.

Cash-flow lens

The $600M 2026 FCF forecast implies an 11.5% yield on estimated enterprise value. But that forecast is external consensus and can fall if the revenue reset persists.

Peer comparisons are deliberately secondary: SSPs report gross revenue differently from DSPs, AppLovin is an app-performance platform rather than a direct DSP comp, and published Viant/PubMatic P/E figures conflict. Apparent precision would be misleading.

07 · Risks & signposts

What the market needs to see next

1

Demand & mix

CPG and autos are about 25% of the business. Weak demand and a shift toward lower-decisioning formats can drag both growth and platform economics.

2

Share loss

Amazon DSP combines commerce signals, owned inventory and discounting. Google, Meta and AppLovin offer simpler automated buying built on proprietary data.

3

Agency leverage

Two holding companies represent 30% of gross spend. The Publicis dispute was settled, but the episode shows how quickly fee transparency can become a commercial issue.

4

Capital allocation

FY2025 buybacks reportedly averaged $52.60 per share, far above the current snapshot. SBC was about 17% of revenue, so repurchases have partly offset dilution rather than created clear value.

5

Execution & people

A new CFO, leadership additions and a 15% workforce reduction raise near-term operating risk. Founder-CEO Jeff Green remains a key-person dependency.

6

Index & positioning

S&P 500 removal effective September 21, 2026 can create passive selling. It is a technical pressure, not a change to cash flows, but it can amplify volatility.

Upgrade conditions

  • Revenue reaccelerates without a lower take rate
  • Kokai/Zuma outcomes translate into durable wallet-share gains
  • Agency relationships stabilize after the Publicis settlement
  • International growth remains near the Q2 pace

Downgrade conditions

  • Q3 falls below the “at least $650M” guide
  • 2027 revenue remains below 2026 or estimates fall again
  • Gross margin trends toward the 70–75% bear range
  • OpenPath or UID2 loses meaningful counterparties
