01 · THE VIEW

A strong asset base, priced for uncertainty

At roughly 7.8× trailing property EBITDAR, Wynn is not expensive. Yet the discount is earned: net leverage is roughly 4–4.4×, Macau supplies about half of group earnings, and the $5.1B Al Marjan project has already absorbed a $600M cost increase and a delay.

The quality

Scarce, high-end resort assets

Wynn Las Vegas generated a 35.1% property EBITDAR margin in 2025, the group’s best. Across markets, the brand monetizes premium rooms, gaming, dining and entertainment in one controlled ecosystem.

The swing factor

UAE can change the earnings base

Management guides to $390–570M of stabilized property EBITDA at Al Marjan. Wynn owns 40%; regulatory first-mover status is powerful, but timing and construction risk have risen.

The constraint

Balance-sheet flexibility

Debt totaled about $10.7B at June 2026. Large maturities in 2027–29 overlap with UAE funding and a $900–950M Macau expansion.

Product quality

Las Vegas combines 4,748 rooms and villas, a 194,000 sq ft casino, 21 dining experiences, 513,000 sq ft of meeting space, retail, nightlife and an 18-hole golf course. This breadth supports premium occupancy and wallet share without relying on a single amenity.

Encore Boston offers 671 rooms, a 211,000 sq ft casino and waterfront public realm; Wynn’s two Macau assets combine more than 2,700 rooms by company disclosure and derivation. Company newsroom

What is no longer the story

WynnBET is effectively wound down. Management exited most US online-sports-betting and iGaming states after concluding that customer-acquisition spending overwhelmed likely returns. Interactive is not part of our valuation case.

Our moat view

Narrow, with unusually valuable assets. Wynn’s advantage is not low cost; it is a hard-to-replicate combination of location, license, brand and service density. The moat is strongest in Las Vegas and potentially the UAE.

It is less durable in Macau, where the concession framework, mandatory non-gaming investment and six-operator field limit strategic freedom. Capital intensity also means that maintaining the brand continuously consumes cash.

Balance sheet

Debt is manageable—but dictates the pace

Total debt was about $10.72B at June 30, 2026, with $1.57B of cash. Management-defined net leverage was about 4.4× in Q1; the exact ratio varies with cash, lease and EBITDA definitions.

The 2027, 2028 and 2029 principal buckets were $2.86B, $2.54B and $1.79B at year-end 2025. A reported $900M 2035 notes offering launched Sept. 10, 2026 seeks to extend the wall, but terms were not verified in an SEC filing for this note.

Capital allocation

Returns continue despite the build cycle

The $0.25 quarterly dividend implies a 1.2% yield at the reference price. Wynn repurchased $358M of stock in 2025 and about $129M in the first half of 2026, leaving $326M authorized at June 30.

That support is useful, but every buyback dollar competes with Al Marjan equity, Enclave capex and refinancing. We prefer opportunistic—not automatic—repurchases until project risk declines.

04 · GROWTH

The UAE is the fulcrum

WYNN AL MARJAN ISLAND · RAS AL KHAIMAH

The first licensed integrated resort in the UAE

A 40%-owned joint venture on a 62-hectare island, carrying a 15-year renewable gaming license exclusive to Ras Al Khaimah. The physical proposition is formidable; the valuation question is whether the cost, geopolitical exposure and time-to-stabilization still produce the guided return.

UAE bull case

First-mover economics

Wynn models $1.0–1.66B of gross gaming revenue and a 16.7–34.3% return on Wynn equity at stabilization. Even the midpoint could add a meaningful new earnings stream.

UAE bear case

Another reset

More delay or cost inflation would hit the project twice: lower present value and more equity cash required, while Wynn guarantees the $2.4B project debt facility.

Portfolio option

70-acre land bank

Additional RAK land creates room for a second phase, but we assign it no value until the first resort opens and economics are demonstrated.

FINAL RECOMMENDATION

Hold. Watch the build, not the headlines.

Wynn owns exceptional resorts and a credible new-market advantage. At $81.68, though, the shares offer only modest upside to our $90 target against a wide $55–123 scenario range. Existing holders can stay for the UAE option; new capital should demand a larger margin of safety.
